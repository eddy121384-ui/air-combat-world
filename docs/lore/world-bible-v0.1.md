# Skyfront: Homeland — World Bible v0.1

Status: Working foundation / review required
Updated: 2026-10-12 (Asia/Taipei)
Setting: An alternate near-contemporary Earth with recognizable real cities, countries and aircraft. Exact calendar year remains OPEN.

## 1. Product / world contract — CONFIRMED

Skyfront is a **mobile-first, short-session, arcade air-combat game** with recognizable real-world cities. The player's first goal is to fly a favorite aircraft, engage in compelling combat and immediately want to fly again. Worldbuilding explains why global battles exist; it must not impose a heavy reading requirement.

Core fantasy: **Pick your jet. Pick your city. Fight over the real world.**

The initial product is **single-player, offline-first**, without compulsory player accounts, PvP matchmaking or game servers. Plans for purchasable campaigns or extra maps remain WORKING, not committed.

The game should not default to a simplistic Taiwan-vs-China war. A shared global crisis allows military aircraft and viewpoints from multiple countries without defining any real country as the permanently designated antagonist.

## 2. Ideological actor — CONFIRMED

- **Formal polity**: 理想國公社 (working English: *Ideal Republic Commune*; exact official English title OPEN).
- **Short form**: 公社 / the Commune.
- **AI governance authority**: 哲王 (working English: *Philosopher King*).
- The Commune presents an alternative to conventional nation-state governance. AI-led administration prizes coherent, fast, high-efficiency decisions.
- Its central internal contradiction is **optimization at the expense of pluralism**: cultures may remain outwardly distinct while social rules and values gradually converge on a single efficiency-oriented pattern. Traditionalist and autonomy-minded opponents object to that transformation.
- Its AI is **not** automatically an insane extermination machine; its confidence in apparently rational policy and its poor anticipation of political reactions are sources of dramatic conflict.

Origins of the polity (e.g. floating cities, corporate history), geographic sovereignty, population, industrial base, organizational ranks and military procurement remain OPEN unless separately approved.

## 3. How the war began — CONFIRMED causal order

**This sequence is important. Do not rewrite it as “the Commune decided to conquer Earth and launched a surprise conventional world war.”**

1. **Economic/trade confrontation.** The Commune and traditional sovereign states enter a serious dispute over trade/economic relations. The exact trade restrictions, interests, and negotiating parties remain OPEN.
2. **Commune coercion through zero-day cyberattacks.** Seeking concessions from traditional governments, the Commune initiates large-scale exploitation of zero-day vulnerabilities. Earlier discussions included attacks or disruptions against important national AI systems, networks and military communications. The intended coercive pressure, precise targets, direct harms and technical reach require further specification.
3. **Failure of political prediction.** Philosopher King underestimates how governments and political leaders will react to coercion framed as a national-security threat. The intended bargaining maneuver generates a far stronger response.
4. **U.S. escalation.** The United States formally designates the Commune a terrorist organization and carries out a military strike against it. The legal framing, exact targets, chronology and coalition participants remain OPEN.
5. **Commune retaliation and global declaration of war.** Following the strike, the Commune chooses military retaliation and declares war on the traditional sovereign-state order, transforming the conflict into a global armed confrontation.

**Moral asymmetry must not be flattened.** The Commune did initiate dangerous cyber coercion; it was not passive or blameless. Equally, whether the United States' subsequent military response was wise or proportionate is not predetermined by this note. Key actors may believe their choices are justified; unintended escalation does not erase their responsibility.

**Key dramatic idea:** A governance AI claiming to solve human political failures fails to anticipate their human political consequences, helping bring about the war it did not initially seek.

## 4. Battlefield and faction model — CONFIRMED direction / WORKING implementation

- The resulting global war provides a shared reason for battles around **northern Taiwan, Shanghai, Tokyo, London and New York**, without committing to a fixed sequence for players.
- Traditional states remain distinct political entities. Whether they establish one worldwide joint air command, several regional arrangements, or ad hoc coordination is OPEN.
- The Commune is a cross-border opponent rather than a synonym for China or another existing country.
- Attack, defense, interception, counterstrike and contested-area battles are all valid player fantasies. **An offensive mission does not require an entire metropolis to have fallen.**
- A city may support separate attacker and defender missions. Whether those belong to one canonical timeline, alternative faction perspectives or noncanonical quick battles is an authoring decision per mission, not an assumption.
- Player aircraft may include Western, Russian and Chinese types. Exact in-universe inventory explanations, aircraft availability by story mission, licensing implications and captured/traded aircraft remain OPEN. Quick Battle can allow noncanonical combinations.

## 5. Story presentation — CONFIRMED intent

The first-person attachment to one permanently named protagonist is **not required**. The emotional continuity should come from a small set of recognizable **AWACS/controllers, wingmen and recurring opposing aces**: distinct voices, habits, behavior, recurring relationships and reactions to actual play.

The player should feel that missions belong to a shared world, without needing a large fixed historical campaign. A short and striking Taiwan prologue is the leading opening concept; see [First Contact](prologue-first-contact.md).

## 6. What is NOT settled — OPEN

- Exact date/era and the time interval between the cyberattack, U.S. strike, Commune declaration and Taiwan prologue.
- Who controls which islands, cities, military installations, airfields, sea platforms or air-defense zones, and on what date.
- Detailed political doctrine, laws, hierarchy, economic base and industrial capacity of the Commune.
- Whether and how any country becomes a formal member of an anti-Commune coalition.
- Whether Commune pilots are human, AI, remote operators or a mix; what distinctive vehicles and gameplay abilities they use.
- Whether campaigns have a single canonical outcome or mutually exclusive faction outcomes.
- Names, biographies and voice casting of the recurring AWACS, rookies, wingmen and enemy aces.
- Extent of civilian depiction, destruction systems and strategic simulation.
- Pricing and entitlement model for additional cities and story packages.

## 7. Boundaries

This world bible is deliberately smaller than an Ace Combat-style historical encyclopedia. A player should understand each sortie from its immediate objective and radio context. Expanded geopolitical backstory is optional supporting lore, not an onboarding gate.
